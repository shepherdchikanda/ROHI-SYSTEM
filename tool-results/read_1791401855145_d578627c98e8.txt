     5→# ROHI SYSTEMS — Mozambique Digital Legal & Regulatory Research
     6→
     7→## 1. The most important finding: Mozambique's framework has changed
     8→
     9→The biggest change is that Mozambique now has a significantly more developed digital-regulation framework than it did even a year ago.
    10→
    11→As of today:
    12→
    13→* **Electronic Transactions Law No. 3/2017** remains foundational.
    14→* **INTIC registration/licensing** for certain electronic-service providers and digital-platform operators is now actively operational.
    15→* **Decree 44/2025** amended the 2023 INTIC regime.
    16→* **Cybersecurity Law No. 13/2026** is now in force.
    17→* **Cybercrime Law No. 14/2026** is now in force.
    18→* A comprehensive **Personal Data Protection Law is still not in force**; the proposal is before the Assembly of the Republic.
    19→* New **Cloud Computing** and **Data Centre** regulations exist.
    20→* Mozambique has created a **National Artificial Intelligence Commission**, but there is not yet a comprehensive AI law equivalent to the EU AI Act.
    21→
    22→That means we should build ROHI's legal architecture **now**, rather than waiting for the data-protection bill.

    26→# 2. Electronic Transactions — Law No. 3/2017
    27→
    28→This is one of the most important laws for ROHI.
    29→
    30→The Electronic Transactions Law applies broadly to public and private persons/entities using ICT in their activities, including electronic and commercial transactions. ([INTIC][1])
    31→
    32→It covers:
    33→
    34→* electronic transactions
    35→* electronic commerce
    36→* electronic communications
    37→* electronic records/messages
    38→* electronic contracts
    39→* electronic marketing
    40→* consumer protection in e-commerce
    41→* electronic payments
    42→* personal-data processing
    43→* intermediary electronic service providers
    44→
    45→### What this means for ROHI
    46→
    47→ROHI's website should be designed assuming that electronic interactions and records can have legal significance.
    48→
    49→So we should maintain:
    50→
    51→* clear identification of ROHI
    52→* accessible Terms
    53→* Privacy Policy
    54→* appropriate consent/notice mechanisms
    55→* records of important transactions
    56→* clear commercial communications
    57→* secure forms
    58→* appropriate security controls
    59→* proper records of client acceptance where online contracting is used
    60→
    61→The law also recognizes evidentiary value in electronic messages, with reliability assessed based on factors such as how information was generated, stored, transmitted and received, and whether its integrity can be maintained. ([Scribd][2])
    62→
    63→That is another reason we should build proper systems for **acceptance, timestamps, versions and records**, rather than simply having a PDF somewhere on the website.

    93→# 4. INTIC registration and licensing
    94→
    95→This is probably the most important issue we need to investigate specifically for ROHI.
    96→
    97→Mozambique's **Decree 59/2023**, as amended by **Decree 44/2025**, establishes registration/licensing for:
    98→
    99→* Intermediate Electronic Service Providers
   100→* Digital Platform Operators
   101→
   102→The regime applies to qualifying services offered to recipients located in Mozambique. ([INTIC][3])
   103→
   104→And this is **not theoretical anymore**.
   105→
   106→INTIC began the mandatory licensing process in December 2025 and has been actively implementing it during 2026. INTIC stated that entities covered by the regime should comply even if they do not have a physical establishment in Mozambique. ([INTIC][4])
   107→
   108→As recently as September/October 2026, INTIC was reporting active licensing and regulatory implementation. ([INTIC][5])
   109→
   110→### But here's the important distinction for ROHI
   111→
   112→I **would not automatically conclude that ROHI's corporate website itself requires an INTIC platform licence.**
   113→
   114→The regulation is aimed at particular categories of electronic intermediaries and digital platforms.
   115→
   116→The original regulation identifies intermediary categories such as:
   117→
   118→* mere conduit
   119→* caching
   120→* hosting
   121→* other approved intermediary services
   122→
   123→and digital-platform categories such as:
   124→
   125→* digital marketplace
   126→* digital search
   127→* digital repository
   128→* digital communication
   129→* digital community
   130→* digital payment
   131→* other approved platforms. ([INTIC][3])
   132→
   133→The 2025 amendment also excludes providers already regulated/licensed under the telecommunications/postal framework. ([INTIC][6])
   134→
   135→### Where ROHI gets interesting
   136→
   137→ROHI isn't simply a marketing website.
   138→
   139→Your planned business includes:
   140→
   141→> custom systems + SaaS/product systems + embedded AI + subscriptions + potentially hosting/managed services.
   142→
   143→If ROHI eventually **operates a SaaS platform on behalf of clients**, hosts data, operates a platform used by third parties, or provides a service falling into one of INTIC's regulated categories, the classification question becomes much more important.
   144→
   145→The 2023 regulation also requires technical information concerning architecture, infrastructure, database location, security and data protection as part of registration requirements. ([INTIC][3])
   146→
   147→### My recommendation
   148→
   149→Before ROHI launches its first commercial platform:
   150→
   151→**Ask INTIC for a written classification/confirmation of which ROHI activities require registration/licensing.**
   152→
   153→Don't guess.
   154→
   155→That gives us much stronger footing than simply putting:
   156→
   157→> "INTIC compliant"
   158→
   159→on the website.

   164→

   166→
   167→* company NUIT
   168→* legal entity certificate
   169→* domain registration evidence
   170→* public IP/ASN information

   202→# 7. What applies to personal data right now?
   203→
   204→There are already important protections.
   205→
   206→## Constitution
   207→
   208→Article 71 of the Constitution protects personal data and restricts certain processing of individually identifiable information. It also gives individuals rights concerning access to data about them and correction. ([Portal do Governo][9])
   209→
   210→## Electronic Transactions Law
   211→
   212→The Electronic Transactions Law contains specific provisions on electronic personal data.
   213→
   214→Among other things, it requires:
   215→
   216→* accuracy
   217→* completeness
   218→* updating
   219→* confidentiality
   220→* specification of the purpose of collection
   221→* identification of the processor
   222→* limiting subsequent use to stated purposes
   223→* protection against unauthorized access, loss, destruction, modification or disclosure
   224→
   225→and provides rights concerning access, information, objection, removal, rectification and completion. ([INTIC][1])
   226→
   227→INTIC itself is actively using these provisions in its regulatory/fiscalisation work. Its current questionnaire specifically asks organizations about:
   228→
   229→* authentication
   230→* prevention of data leaks
   231→* incident response
   232→* cookies/tracking
   233→* whether users are informed
   234→* whether users can manage privacy preferences. ([INTIC][10])
   235→
   236→That is extremely relevant to ROHI.

   381→# 12. Consumer protection
   382→
   383→Mozambique has the **Consumer Protection Law No. 22/2009**, supplemented by **Decree No. 27/2016**, which regulates consumer protection.
   384→
   385→The law protects consumer rights including:
   386→
   387→* quality
   388→* safety
   389→* information
   390→* economic interests
   391→* compensation
   392→* protection against misleading/abusive advertising. ([Oportunidades][12])
   393→
   394→But there is an important distinction for ROHI:
   395→
   396→### A business customer isn't automatically a "consumer."
   397→
   398→The legal concept depends on the transaction and whether the acquisition is for non-professional use. ([MDR Advogados Mozambique][13])
   399→
   400→Since ROHI's core market is businesses buying technology for business purposes, much of ROHI's commercial activity will be **B2B** rather than consumer commerce.
   401→
   402→Nevertheless, your public website should still follow consumer-grade principles:
   403→
   404→> clear, accurate, understandable, non-deceptive information.
   405→
   406→And if ROHI ever launches a consumer-facing product, the consumer regime becomes much more important.

   411→
   412→If ROHI eventually sells subscriptions directly online — which is likely given the AI/subscription model we're designing — the Electronic Transactions Law becomes especially relevant.
